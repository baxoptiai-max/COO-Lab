Stage 0 · AI-Native Baseline

COO Lab

Why it matters: A COO needs meeting rhythm, decision rules and a route choice before any tool is added.

The choice: Run on the tools and AI provider the company already has, with a neutral schema.

The trade-off: It is a pattern, not a product to install; the workspace and tools are assumed, not known.

A working example of AI-native operations for a COO at a three-year-old bio-AI company: leadership decisions become a decision log and an action tracker, and the all-hands becomes an always-on page that leadership updates every week and everyone can ask questions. No decks or documents to send around.

Built by Barbara van Asten for the AI Operations / Chief of Staff application at Bioptimus. I have no view of Bioptimus's internal tools, so COO Lab is a pattern, not a product to install: it runs on the stack a company already has. All example data on this page is fictional.

Each leader posts a weekly status update on the all-hands page before the meeting. Meeting time is for decisions; nothing is distributed as a file.

Decision rules

  1. Every decision has one owner and a due date.
  2. Every decision names the goal or OKR it moves.
  3. The trade-off is written down in one line.
  4. Every decision carries a label: All-hands, Leadership only or Board only.
  5. A changed decision gets a new entry; the old one stays visible.

AI fluency curriculum (non-R&D teams)

  1. Build your own workflow: each person automates one recurring task from their own week.
  2. Sensitive data: what may go into which tool, and why the labels above exist.
  3. Check the output: test cases, sources and when a human signs off.

Success measure: workflows still in use after 90 days, not attendance.

Module 1 · Decision to action

From leadership meeting to action

Every decision gets an owner, a date, a link to a company goal and a confidentiality label. Nothing is published before the COO approves it.

Module 2 · The all-hands page (Extend with RAG)

An always-on all-hands page you can ask

The all-hands is no longer a deck. It is one live page with vision, mission, targets, delivery status, the decision log and each leader's weekly status update. In the weekly all-hands, leadership walks through what changed on the page; between meetings, anyone can ask it a question and get an answer with its source.

What the page can search depends on who is asking. Access is filtered before the model reads anything, so a colleague's question never reaches leadership-only text.

Module 3 · Weekly status update

How the weekly updates get onto the page

Leaders don't start from a blank page. The leadership meeting transcript already holds most of their status, so COO Lab drafts each leader's update from it. The leader then adds or corrects by speaking instead of typing: a Stanford study found speech input about three times faster than typing on a phone keyboard (Stanford, 2016).

Why it matters: The COO sees what a meeting produces before anything reaches the company.

The choice: The model extracts, code checks, and the all-hands text uses only agreed items labelled all_hands.

The trade-off: The leak check can withhold a harmless paragraph; the COO reads it and releases it after review.

    KPI and measurement scorecard

    One scorecard across R&D, Commercial and Operations. The decision log supplies the first operations metrics: time from decision to done, and the share of decisions with an owner and date.

    Research-to-delivery handoff

    Gates between exploratory research and the teams that ship to enterprise customers. Each gate is a decision type, so it follows the same decision rules and lands in the same log.

    Context and assumptions

    Why this matters for a bio-AI company

    Bioptimus states it works with 16 of the top 20 pharma companies and 1,000+ institutions, and that 60% of its team hold PhDs (bioptimus.com). Its models are for research use only and are deployed on AWS SageMaker or on-premise (documentation). A company running exploratory research next to enterprise delivery needs decisions that are light on process and clear on ownership.

    Assumptions

    1. Leadership meetings are recorded and transcribed, with consent.
    2. There is a weekly leadership meeting and a weekly all-hands.
    3. Confidentiality labels do not exist yet; the decision rules introduce them.
    4. AI fluency training covers legal, HR, finance, commercial ops and the internal AI ops team.
    5. The workspace, meeting tool and AI provider are unknown; COO Lab uses a neutral schema that works with any of them.
